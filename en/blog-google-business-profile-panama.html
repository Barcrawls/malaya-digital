Google Business Profile in Panama: the 2026 guide to showing up on the map

By  Ciro Jaén  · Founder of MALAYA · Operator of experience businesses in Panama and Colombia
Direct answer: the Google Business Profile is the free listing that makes your business appear on Google Maps and in search results. For it to work, it needs four things: completed verification, the right category, complete and consistent information, and constant activity — photos, posts and answered reviews. This guide covers every step.

What is it and why does it matter so much?

When someone searches "coffee tasting in Casco Viejo" or "Canal tour", Google shows the map with three featured businesses first — the famous "local pack" — before any website. If your business isn't there, for a large share of your potential customers you simply don't exist. The profile is also where your reviews live: your most valuable trust asset.

How do I create and verify it?

  • Go to business.google.com with the business's Google account (not an employee's personal one — profiles get lost that way).
  • First search whether your business already appears on the map: if it exists, claim it instead of creating a duplicate.
  • Complete verification: Google usually asks for a video showing the venue, the operation and a business document. Have your operating notice or registration at hand.
record the verification video calmly and in good light: showing the storefront, the interior and branded materials. Rejections are almost always due to rushed videos that don't prove the business is real.

How do I choose categories?

The main category is the profile's most strategic decision: it defines which searches you can appear in. The rule: choose your star product's category, not your venue's description. A business whose main income is guided experiences performs better as "Tour operator" than as "Coffee shop", even if it serves coffee. Secondary categories cover the rest of what you do.

What information must be complete?

  • Real hours — including holidays. An incorrect "Closed" on a Saturday costs a tourism business real customers.
  • Services and products with prices — they appear directly on the listing and filter out the merely curious.
  • Booking link — pointing to your booking page, not your generic homepage.
  • Your own, recent photos — no stock imagery: Google and customers can tell the difference. Refresh them monthly.
  • Data identical to your website — name, address and phone, character by character.

What do I do about reviews?

Two things, without exception. First, ask for them systematically: the best moment is at the close of the experience, when the excitement is high — a physical QR code works better than any follow-up email. Second, answer all of them, good and bad, within 48 hours: it signals activity to Google and trust to the next customer evaluating you. Never buy reviews or set up exchanges: platforms detect them and the penalty can be profile suspension.

And after setting it up, then what?

The profile isn't paperwork: it's a channel. Post updates (a new edition, an event, a promotion), upload fresh photos, update holiday hours, and check the monthly stats: how many saw you, how many called, how many asked for directions. Those numbers are your first visibility thermometer — and the basis for deciding what to improve next.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Google Business Profile free?

Yes, completely. Creating it, verifying it, posting and answering reviews costs nothing. Be wary of anyone charging you to "register you on Google".

Can I have a profile if I work by appointment only or have no storefront?

Yes. Google allows service-area businesses (no visible address) and appointment-only operations. The key is configuring the right type from the start.

What if my business already appears but I don't manage it?

Claim it from business.google.com using "Own this business?". After verifying your identity, you get control of the listing.

How often should I post?

At least once a month; ideally weekly. Constant activity signals to Google that the business is alive.
