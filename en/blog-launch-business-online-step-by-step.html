Step-by-step guide to launching your business online (without getting lost along the way)

By  Ciro Jaén  · Founder of MALAYA · Operator of experience businesses in Panama and Colombia
Direct answer: launching a business online has an order that genuinely matters. First you define what you sell and to whom; then you set up your site and your domain; next you build your presence where your customers already search (Google, Bing, Apple Maps); in parallel you start accumulating reviews from your very first customer; and finally you measure everything to know what's actually working. Skipping steps doesn't save time — it costs it later, when you have to go back and fix what was done wrong from the start.

This guide was born from a question we get all the time: "where do I start?" There's no textbook answer — there's an order that works, learned by operating our own experience businesses in Panama and Colombia, and those of the clients we serve at MALAYA. Here are the eight steps, in the order that actually matters.

01Define what you sell and to whom, in a single sentence

Before registering a domain or opening an account, write one sentence: "I sell [what] to [whom]". It sounds obvious, but it's the step most businesses skip — and the one that costs them the most later. Without that clear sentence, you end up picking the wrong category on Google, writing a site that speaks to nobody in particular, and competing against businesses that aren't really your competition.

if you can't state your business in a ten-word sentence, you're not ready for step 2 yet. The extra day of thinking is worth it.

02Register your domain and put up a simple site

Your own domain (yourbusiness.com, not a free page with another platform's name in the URL) is yours — it doesn't depend on anyone else's rules or algorithm. It doesn't need to be complex: one page with what you do, how to contact you, and evidence that you're real (photos, reviews if you have them) is enough to start.

What does matter from day one, even if the site is simple: every page needs a title and description written with what your customer searches in mind — not how you'd describe your business internally.

03Create and verify your Google Business Profile

This is, without exaggeration, the highest-impact-for-least-effort step on the whole list. The Google Business Profile is free, and it's what appears on the map and in the side panel when someone searches for your type of business near them. A new business without this verified profile is, for practical purposes, invisible on Google — no matter how good the product is.

choose the main category based on your star product (not your venue in general), complete every section, and upload real photos. Verification can take from minutes to a few days depending on the method Google offers you. It is by far the block of work that returns results fastest — which is why it's the first thing we fix when someone asks us to tune up a business.

04Get on the platforms where your customers already search

Google isn't the only door. iPhone users search on Apple Maps. Bing feeds the answers of several AI assistants. And depending on your industry, there are specific directories (booking, tourism, dining) where your customers are already searching for exactly what you offer. Being only on Google means leaving doors closed that cost nothing to open.

register your business on Bing Places and Apple Business Connect — both free, and many allow importing your data directly from your already-loaded Google Business Profile, which shortens the process to minutes.

05Build reputation from your first customer

Reviews are the number one decision factor for a new customer who doesn't know you — and a direct ranking signal for Google. The most common mistake is waiting until you "have enough customers" to start asking. The most valuable review is usually the first one: the one that breaks a new profile's silence.

ask for the review at the exact moment the customer is happiest (right after the experience, not days later by email), and answer every one — good or bad — within 48 hours. It sounds simple, but it's one of the first steps that gets abandoned when the business grows and nobody has time to stay on top of it; that's usually where a third party comes in to sustain it.

06Keep your data identical everywhere

If your site says one address, your Google Business Profile another, and your Instagram an old phone number, Google loses trust in your information — literally the criterion it uses to decide who to show first. This is called NAP consistency (name, address, phone), and it's a silent error: nobody warns you that you have it, you just stop showing up.

make a list of every place your business exists online, and compare name, address and phone character by character. Ten minutes that prevent months of confusion for Google.

07Measure from launch, not six months later

Google Search Console and Google Analytics are free, and they tell you exactly what's happening: how many people find you, what they search to reach you, and which pages work. Installing them takes less than fifteen minutes — and without them, every decision you make about your online presence is a guess, not a fact.

We did it with this very site: Search Console and Analytics were live the same day as the launch, not as an improvement for "later". That way, from the first week, we know what's working instead of guessing.

08Publish, measure, repeat — with real data, not intuition

The launch isn't the finish line, it's the starting point. Once the previous seven steps are in motion, the work becomes a simple cycle: check what the data says, adjust what isn't working, and keep going.

Real case

What happens when you actually measure

With Panama Barcrawl, one of our own businesses, Search Console data showed growth from 149 to 651 clicks — a +337% — beating the business's all-time record by +117%, even in low season. The share of searches by brand name versus generic searches went from 0% to 75% non-brand: the clearest signal that the business stopped depending only on people who already knew it, and started appearing in front of new people searching for the experience without knowing the name yet. None of those numbers would exist without measuring from the start.

That's the complete cycle: the first seven steps build the presence, and the eighth keeps it alive. A business that launches following this order doesn't depend on luck to get found — it depends on every piece being where it should be. We follow exactly these eight steps with every business we launch, our own and those of the people who hire us — sometimes the difference isn't knowing what to do, but having someone to sustain it while you run the business.

Frequently asked questions

Where should I start if I'm launching a new business?

By defining in one sentence what you sell and to whom — before touching any platform. Everything else gets decided faster and better once that sentence is clear.

Do I need a website before showing up on Google?

It's not mandatory to start: your Google Business Profile can be active without your own site. But a site, even a simple one, gives you a place of your own that doesn't depend on any platform's rules.

How long does it take for a new business to start showing up online?

The Google Business Profile can be visible on the map within days once verified. Site rankings and accumulated reviews take longer — usually between 4 and 12 weeks of consistency before clear results.

Can I do all of this myself or do I need help?

You can do it alone — it's not technically complicated, it takes time and consistency. Professional help speeds up the process and avoids mistakes that cost months of lost visibility, but no step in this guide requires knowledge you can't learn.
